## Scope

The roadmap's first cut, plus what planning turned up:

- **Redaction:**
  - Mark areas, text or whole pages.
  - Export a copy with **true content removal**: text, OCR text, images,
    line art, and annotations under the marks. Optional fill color and
    overlay text.
- **Search and redact:** Patterns hits, word lists and OCR text become
  redaction marks, covering whole words or the characters you choose,
  as Acrobat does.
- **Remove hidden information:** metadata, attachments, comments, form
  fields, hidden layers, content outside the crop box, links, actions and
  JavaScript, search indexes, thumbnails, and sheaf's own private data.
  Export Clean Copy becomes one preset of it.
- **Encryption kept on save.** Done in M10.5 (slices 7, 9 and 10): every
  save, Append, Rewrite, Save As and recovery sidecar, keeps the file's
  encryption. What's left is slice 1's.
- **Passwords and permissions:** set, change and remove. New encryption is
  AES-256.
- **Moved out:**
  - Certificate encryption moves to after M12 (decision 3).
  - The leak through deleted pages is fixed in M9 (decision 1).

## Decided while planning (Greg, 2026-10-14)

1. **Deleting has to delete, and that's fixed in M9.** Today a deleted
   page stays in the saved file whenever anything still points at it.
   Inserting one page from another file can bring that whole file along.
   An incremental save keeps everything removed since the last save in
   the earlier revision.
   - Reproduced on `f61e7eb`; the details are bug 14 in
     [`m9.md`](m9.md).
   - Greg added it to M9 as slice 21, so v0.5 doesn't ship it. Done in
     `109823d`.
   - Redaction builds on it: a redacted page replaces the original, and
     the original must not survive in the file.
2. **Permissions are shown and kept, not enforced.**
   - A file with only a permissions password (bank statements that
     forbid editing or copying) opens as today, and you can still mark
     it up.
   - Properties lists the restrictions, and a one-time notice says the
     file has them.
   - Saves keep them, and Security… can remove them once you give the
     permissions password.
   - ISO 32000 says permissions are advisory, and qpdf and pdf.js don't
     enforce them either.
3. **Certificate encryption (`/Adobe.PubSec`) comes after M12.**
   - PDFium only opens the standard (password) handler, so sheaf would
     have to decrypt these files itself.
   - It also needs certificate stores (PKCS#12, the Windows store,
     PKCS#11), which M12 builds.
   - Until then, sheaf says clearly that a file is certificate-encrypted
     and can't be opened.
4. **Applying redactions writes a copy; the binder keeps its marks.**
   Greg leaned toward exporting, but worried that people would believe
   they'd redacted something and send the original. Exporting comes with
   guard rails aimed at exactly that, and he agreed to them:
   - **Marks never look applied.** An unapplied mark is drawn as a
     hatched outline labeled "Redaction (not applied)", never as a filled
     box. A Preview toggle shows the page as the copy will look, with a
     banner while it's on. (Acrobat also shows outlines until you apply.)
   - **The document says so.** While a document has unapplied marks, a
     bar above the page says "3 redactions not applied", with an Export
     Redacted Copy… button.
   - **Every way out of the binder asks first:** Save As, the exports
     (pages, text, flattened, clean copy), Print, Extract, Split and
     Combine. "This sends the text under 3 redaction marks. Export a
     redacted copy instead?" The default button is the redacted copy.
     It's a gate of the export preflight (M10.5 §12.1), which every
     export goes through, and of Save As's plan.
   - **Applying is a `Transform` on an `ExportTx`** (M10.5 §12, §20):
     only an export takes one, and `Document::commit` takes a `Tx`,
     which can't hold one, so applying in place, by a plugin or
     anyone, is impossible by type.
   - **The copy is unmistakable.**
     - It's named `<name>_Redacted.pdf` by default (Acrobat's suffix).
     - It opens in its own window when written, with "Redacted copy" by
       the title.
     - It has no unapplied marks.
     - It comes with a report (proposal 9).
   - **The file itself warns.** The binder saves its marks as standard
     `/Redact` annotations, so a recipient's Acrobat or sheaf shows them
     as unapplied redactions, not as black boxes.
   - **No apply-in-place in M11,** so there's one model to learn. It can
     be added later, using the same engine.

## Proposed (to confirm at kickoff)

5. **Redaction marks are standard `/Redact` annotations.**
   - Written with `QuadPoints`, `IC` (fill), `OverlayText`, `DA`, `Q` and
     `Repeat`, plus a generated `/RO` and `/AP`. So Acrobat can review
     and apply sheaf's marks, and the reverse.
   - A third-party `/Redact` opens as an editable sheaf redaction mark,
     not as a comment (bug 7).
   - Marks also keep the `/SheafAnnot` JSON, as every sheaf mark does
     (M9 decision 1).
6. **Our own content interpreter and rewriter, on lopdf. PDFium only
   finds and checks.**
   - PDFium has no redaction API, and its page-object editing can't
     remove single glyphs.
   - Its `FPDFPage_GenerateContent` isn't safe to rely on:
     - it drops shadings and inline images;
     - it writes CMYK, Separation and Pattern colors as black;
     - it drops `Tc`, `Tw`, `Tz`, `TL` and `Ts`;
     - it leaves an unbalanced `q BT` on Type3 text;
     - it rewrites a shared Form XObject in place, changing every page
       that uses it.
   - So the interpreter lives in `sheaf-doc` (the roadmap's shared
     infrastructure for M13 and M14). PDFium renders and extracts text
     from the output to check it.
   - It builds on M10.5's `sheaf-doc/src/content/` (its lexer, splicer,
     `Balance` and `TextBag`; M10.5 §13), not on lopdf's `Content`,
     which M10.5 retired (its proposal 17).
7. **What's removed under a mark.**
   - **Text:** every glyph whose box, shrunk by 10% on each side, overlaps
     the mark. Shrinking keeps neighboring letters from being caught by
     ascenders and side bearings (MuPDF does the same).
     - Text in any render mode counts, invisible OCR text included.
     - Glyphs that survive are written out again with absolute positions
       (`Tm` per run), not TJ offsets.
     - A 2023 study (PETS) recovered removed text from the offsets of
       surviving glyphs in 11 tools, Acrobat among them. Absolute
       positions avoid that.
     - The gap a mark leaves still shows the removed text's length. That
       can't be avoided without reflowing, and the docs say so.
   - **Images:**
     - An image wholly under a mark is removed.
     - A partly covered image is decoded, its pixels under the mark are
       replaced with the fill color, and it's encoded again.
       PDFium decodes, since it handles every filter. Bilevel images
       become CCITT G4 (`sheaf-image`), JPEGs become JPEG again, and the
       rest Flate.
     - An image shared with other pages is cloned first.
     - Soft masks and stencil masks are cleared under the mark. Inline
       images are handled the same way.
   - **Line art:**
     - Paths wholly under a mark are removed.
     - Partly covered paths are clipped geometrically, keeping what's
       outside the mark. Curves are flattened only in the paths that get
       clipped, with a polygon-boolean crate such as `geo` or `i_overlay`
       (licenses to check).
     - A clip path can't be used instead, because it leaves the data in
       the file.
   - **Shadings:** removed when wholly under a mark, otherwise kept. A
     known limit, as in MuPDF, and documented.
   - **Form XObjects:** a copy is made for each use that a mark touches,
     and the copy is redacted. Other uses keep the original.
   - **Annotations:** any annotation that overlaps a mark is removed:
     links, comments and sheaf marks. A widget that overlaps loses its
     widget and the value of its field.
   - **Text kept beside the content:**
     - `/ActualText` and `/Alt` that span removed glyphs, in marked
       content and in the structure tree;
     - the words of OCR text layers under the mark;
     - `/ToUnicode` and `/Widths` entries for character codes no longer
       used anywhere in the document;
     - the `/Thumb` of a redacted page.
   - **Whole pages:** Redact Pages… marks a page range. Each page becomes
     a blank page of the same size, with the fill and overlay text and no
     annotations.
8. **Leak check across the document.** When a redacted copy is written,
   each removed string (the text under every mark) is searched for in:
   - every remaining page's text;
   - bookmark titles and annotation contents, sheaf's own included (a
     Replace mark's struck text);
   - metadata, form values, page labels, named destinations, link URIs
     and attachment names.

   Hits are listed, and the export offers to remove them. A bookmark made
   by "Bookmark matches" on an SSN is the obvious case.

   **It runs over the output, not `DocState`** (M10.5 §20): a walk of the
   emission (built values, and the source objects reachable from the
   roots), with content and appearance strings decoded, plus the text
   service over the written bytes. It covers Info and XMP, embedded-file
   names, named destinations, link URIs and Launch targets, structure
   `/Alt` and `/ActualText`, `/TU`, JavaScript, `/PieceInfo` and
   `/SheafAnnot`. A pass over `DocState` is only a fast pre-check.
9. **Verification, every time.**
   - sheaf reopens the copy it wrote with PDFium and checks that no
     character box intersects a mark.
   - When a pattern made the marks, it checks that the pattern no longer
     matches inside them.
   - The report says what was removed: text runs, images edited, paths
     clipped, annotations removed, leak-check changes. It also says what
     couldn't be checked.
   - A failed check stops the export and names the page.
10. **Encryption.**
    - Every save keeps a file's encryption exactly: rewrite, incremental,
      recovery, Save As. "Password protection removed" goes away.
      - It reuses the file's `/Encrypt` dictionary and file key, so no
        owner password is needed.
      - `/ID` doesn't change, since R2–R4 keys depend on it.
    - **Kept on every save since M10.5** (slices 7, 9 and 10):
      `serialize/crypt.rs` writes each object with the file's key, and
      the recovery sidecar is encrypted as the file is.
    - New encryption is AES-256 (R6) only. PDF 2.0 deprecates RC4 and
      AES-128.
    - **New AES-256 encryption (V5, R6) writes `/Length 256` in
      `/Encrypt`,** in M11's encryption code or as a `vendor/lopdf`
      patch: lopdf's `Document::encrypt` leaves it out, and poppler 26.01
      then garbles every stream. Its tests check new AES-256 output with
      poppler (M10.5 slice 2's notes).
    - All Standard-handler revisions can still be read.
    - Encryption is removed only through Security…, or `--decrypt` in
      the CLI.
11. **No plaintext copies of an encrypted document on disk.** Mostly met
    by M10.5: generated sources are memory blobs (the generated cache is
    gone), and the recovery sidecar is encrypted. What's left is the
    attachments folder (slice 1).
    - Its generated sources (crops, watermarks, stripped copies) stay in
      memory as `SourceData`; slice 4 of M9 already keeps every source's
      bytes there.
    - Its recovery section is encrypted with the document's key.
    - Temporary files are deleted when the document closes: the
      recovered copy, and attachments opened from the panel.
12. **Remove Hidden Information… is one engine.**
    - A dialog of categories that writes a copy. Acrobat's list, plus
      sheaf's own:
      - metadata (Info, XMP, `/PieceInfo`);
      - attachments;
      - bookmarks;
      - comments and markup;
      - form fields (removed or flattened);
      - hidden text that isn't OCR;
      - hidden layers (their content removed through `/OC`);
      - content outside the crop box (redacted as the area outside it);
      - links, actions and JavaScript (`/OpenAction`, `/AA`,
        `/Names /JavaScript`, Launch, URIs);
      - embedded search indexes;
      - thumbnails;
      - sheaf's private data (`SheafSettings`, which holds a watermark
        image's local path; `/SheafHeaderFooter`; `/SheafAnnot`
        payloads).
    - Unreferenced objects always go, by a full rewrite.
    - Export Redacted Copy offers it as a checkbox, on by default, with
      the categories remembered in `state.json`.
    - Export Clean Copy becomes a preset with its current behavior,
      since PDFlyer users expect it.
    - Not in M11: Acrobat's "overlapping objects" (objects hidden behind
      others). It needs occlusion analysis; ❓ for M14.
13. **Search and redact through the Patterns panel.**
    - "Mark for Redaction" on the hits, beside "Bookmark matches": Check
      All or one at a time.
    - Word and phrase lists, alongside regular expressions.
    - **Whole or partial words, as Acrobat offers them** (decided by Greg,
      2026-10-14).
      - Acrobat's Redaction Mark Options: whole words, or partial words
        with a settings dialog for how many characters to redact and
        where they sit in the word.
      - sheaf does the same: a general setting for any search, not one
        tied to a kind of number. For example, all but four characters
        at the end, or the first five.
      - The mark covers just those characters' boxes, from PDFium's
        per-character boxes, or the OCR layer's for scanned pages.
      - Whole words is the default, and the last setting is remembered in
        `state.json`.
      - The docs say what a partial mark leaves visible. Leaving the last
        four digits of an SSN is common (26 CFR 301.6109-4 allows it on
        payee statements). But for SSNs issued before the 2011
        randomization, Acquisti and Gross (PNAS, 2009) showed the first
        five digits can often be predicted from birth date and state. So
        the last four next to a date of birth come close to the whole
        number.
    - New presets: email, credit card (with a Luhn check), ITIN.
    - It searches OCR text, which Acrobat's Search & Redact can't
      (image-only text).
14. **Command line.**
    - `sheaf redact` applies the marks in a file, or a pattern, to a
      copy.
    - `sheaf sanitize` removes hidden information into a copy.
    - `sheaf protect` sets passwords and permissions; `--decrypt` removes
      them.
    - Each prints the same report as the app. M15's folder batches build
      on these.
15. **Plugins can place redaction marks but never apply them.** Applying
    needs the user, through Export Redacted Copy, and is a `Transform`,
    which no plugin edit can hold. Redaction marks join the WIT `annot`
    kinds in the one API bump at the end of M11 (0.6, since M10 takes
    0.5), and the ops plugins submit (a new `edit-schema` minor).
16. **Signatures in a redacted or sanitized copy are removed,** and the
    report says so. They would be invalid anyway, since the copy is a
    full rewrite. M12 then deals with removal and signing in the binder.

## Found while planning

Bugs and gaps in today's code that matter for M11. File and function
references are as of `f61e7eb`. The slice in brackets fixes each one.

1. **Deleted pages stay in the saved file.** Moved to M9 as bug 14 and
   slice 21 (decision 1).
2. **Encryption is dropped without a word.**
   - Every load removes `/Encrypt` and clears `encryption_state`
     (`file::load_pdf`, `file::load_source`).
   - Incremental saves bail on encrypted files, and the rewrite writes
     plaintext.
   - When the user typed a password, the notice says "password
     protection removed".
   - For a file with only a permissions password, lopdf decrypts on its
     own (`Document::load` handles an empty user password), so there's
     no notice at all. The permissions are lost too.

   [Fixed in M10.5, slices 3 and 7: sources keep their encryption, and
   every save writes it.]
3. **Properties shows no security for those files.** It asks
   `is_encrypted()`, which is false once lopdf has decrypted
   (`properties.rs`). [Slice 1.]
4. **No crash protection for owner-only encrypted files.** Recovery
   is skipped only when a password was typed (`docview.rs`, the autosave
   path). For these files `incremental_section` bails, so every autosave
   just logs a warning. [Fixed in M10.5 by the encrypted sidecar, slice
   10.]
5. **Plaintext copies left on disk:**
   - the generated cache (`generated.rs`): decrypted crops, watermarks
     and stripped copies, never deleted (`m8.md`);
   - the recovered temp file `sheaf-recovered-<pid>-<ms>.pdf`, never
     removed (`docview.rs`);
   - attachments opened from the panel, written to
     `$TMP/sheaf-attachments-<pid>/` and never removed
     (`attachments_panel.rs`).

   [The generated cache and the recovered temp file: fixed in M10.5
   (slices 9 and 19). The attachments folder: slice 1.]
6. **Patterns searches only the primary source** (`pattern_panel.rs`,
   via `index_of_source_page`).
   - Inserted, cropped, watermarked, blank and image pages give no hits
     until saved and reopened.
   - Duplicated pages get hits on the first copy only.

   Pattern-driven redaction would miss them. [Fixed in M10.5 by the text
   service, slice 14, which reads every page as it draws.]
7. **Acrobat's redaction marks are treated as comments**
   (`is_comment_subtype` in `file.rs`).
   - Export Clean Copy deletes them.
   - Flatten burns in their outline and removes nothing.
   - Nothing says the file has unapplied redactions.

   [Slice 4.]
8. **Copies of page text live outside the content stream:**
   - a Replace mark's `struck` text and mark contents in `/SheafAnnot`
     (`annot.rs`);
   - bookmark titles from "Bookmark matches" (`pattern_panel.rs`);
   - OCR layer streams (`/SheafOcrLayer`);
   - `/ActualText` and `/Alt` in the structure tree.

   A content rewriter alone wouldn't reach them. [Slices 5 and 7, by
   proposals 7 and 8.]
9. **Hidden data in saved files:**
   - watermark settings with the image's local path in content property
     lists (`watermark.rs`, `SheafSettings`);
   - header/footer settings in the catalog;
   - `/Info`, XMP and `/PieceInfo` kept as they were, with `ModDate` and
     `Producer` never updated.

   All are fine in a binder, but sanitizing must remove them. [Slice 9.
   M10.5 no longer writes the watermark settings or the image's local
   path (slice 16), and Clean Copy strips sheaf's private data,
   `private::KEYS` (slice 19).]
10. **Gaps in lopdf 0.45:**
    - strings inside stream dictionaries are neither decrypted nor
      encrypted (issue #603), for example an Indexed palette;
    - an owner password on an R2–R4 file silently gives the wrong key
      (#584);
    - `encrypt()` clears `encryption_state`, so encrypt a clone;
    - the README's "empty passwords only" is stale.

    Work around them in sheaf, and send fixes upstream. [Slice 1. #603
    is closed in M10.5 by its lazy reader and `serialize/crypt.rs`
    (slices 3 and 7); #584 is left for slice 1.]
11. **The incremental writer can't encrypt.** It writes plaintext objects
    and no `/Encrypt` in the trailer (`incremental.rs`, the hand-written
    object writer). [Fixed in M10.5, slices 3 and 7: one serializer
    writes every save, encrypted.]
12. **The legacy `PdfFile::open` / `save_as`** (`file.rs`) uses lopdf
    directly and would write plaintext too. Remove it, or route it
    through the save path. [Slice 1; M10.5 left it untouched
    (file.rs:10).]
13. **The only content rewriter is narrow.** `stamps::strip` removes
    tagged artifacts from page `/Contents`. It doesn't descend into Form
    XObjects or appearances and has no geometry. Crop only sets
    `/CropBox`, so content outside it stays. [Slice 3. M10.5 deleted
    `stamps::strip` (slice 17); its lexical splice in `content/` (slices
    6 and 16) is what slice 3's rewriter builds on. Slice 3 built the
    interpreter, with geometry and every form use, and its writer
    (`interp::encode`); rewriting with them is slices 5 and 6's, and
    content outside the crop box proposal 12's (slice 9).]

Not a disk leak, recorded so nobody looks again: `Document.sources` and
history keep unredacted bytes in memory for the session, for undo. That's
intended. The redacted copy is a separate file, built only from the marks'
output.

## Oracles (Greg, 2026-10-23)

The leak check's verification reads each redacted copy with several
readers at once: PDFium, pdf.js's text layer and poppler's `pdftotext`,
plus a byte search of `qpdf --qdf` output. Text one reader's quirks hide
is found by another ([docs/oracles.md](../docs/oracles.md#by-milestone)).

     strictly as `content::marked` does: a `BDC` whose named list
     `/Properties` lacks (or that is neither a name nor a dictionary)
     opens nothing, so its `EMC` closes the range around it; a stray
     `EMC` is passed over; a range left open ends where its content
     does, at an empty operator at its end; a form's `EMC`s can't end
     its drawer's ranges. Marked points keep their list.

     `Summary.refused`, never a page error: past 40 deep, as PDFium skips
     it; within itself, at once, where PDFium draws it. PDFium's own
     cycle check compares the buffers it reads each use into, which it
     makes afresh for every use of a stream read from a file, filtered or
     not (`CPDF_StreamAcc` copies a file's stream), so it draws a
     self-drawing form 40 deep: a cycle refused means the interpreter
     reports less than PDFium draws (`Refused::Cycle` says so), and slice
     5 fails closed on any refusal.

     per page, fonts' streams within the page's budget, and geometry an
     `f32` can't hold an error where PDFium carries infinities on. None
     comes near a corpus page (tier 1: 17,864,912 bytes, 60× under its
     cap; 995,739 segments, 8.4×; no page fails).

     absolute `Td`,** not one `Tm` with the line folded in (as planned):
     PDFium places the line by the same sums then, to the bit; the fold
     re-rounded and moved 120 corpus pages. A `Td` that moves nothing is
     written as it is: any operator ends PDFium's reading of path
     operators after an `m`, and dropping it drew `100 100 m Td l S` as
     a line (the proptest).

     ignores `usecmap` and `/UseCMap`; sheaf follows the stream chain.

     descriptor with a `/FontBBox` that isn't all 0, with `Ascent` and

     `approx.heights`, the
